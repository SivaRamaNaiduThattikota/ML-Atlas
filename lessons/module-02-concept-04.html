MODULE 02 · CONCEPT 04

ROC-AUC
and PR-AUC.

Concept 03 computed precision and recall at one fixed threshold and stopped there. This concept moves that threshold — every possible value of it — and traces two curves as it moves: the ROC curve (true positive rate against false positive rate) and the PR curve (precision against recall). The area under each curve, ROC-AUC and PR-AUC, summarizes a model across every threshold at once instead of just the one Concept 03 froze.

01 · CONCEPT FIRST

Two curves, traced by sweeping one threshold

A model that outputs a continuous risk score, not just a yes/no, can be thresholded anywhere. Sweep that cutoff from "flag nobody" to "flag everybody" and plot two different pairs of numbers at every stop: TPR against FPR gives the ROC curve; precision against recall gives the PR curve. The area under each curve — ROC-AUC and PR-AUC — is one number summarizing the whole sweep, not just Concept 03's single frozen point.

First memory: ROC-AUC and PR-AUC summarize every threshold at once; Concept 03's precision/recall described exactly one of them.
02 · WHY IT EXISTS

One threshold can flatter or bury a model unfairly

Concept 03's numbers depend entirely on which threshold happened to be chosen. A model that's genuinely strong could look weak at a badly-picked cutoff, and vice versa. Sweeping every threshold removes that dependency — ROC-AUC and PR-AUC describe how good the model's ranking is, independent of where anyone eventually decides to draw the line.

ONE THRESHOLD (CONCEPT 03)

Precision and recall describe exactly one operating point — change the cutoff, get different numbers.

EVERY THRESHOLD (THIS CONCEPT)

ROC-AUC and PR-AUC describe the model's ranking quality across all possible cutoffs at once.

04 · TECHNICAL MECHANISM

Same sweep, two different pairs of ratios plotted

ROC CURVE

TPR (y) vs. FPR (x)

TPR is Concept 03's recall, renamed here; FPR = FP/(FP+TN), the fraction of real non-relief complaints wrongly flagged.

PR CURVE

Precision (y) vs. recall (x)

The same two ratios Concept 03 computed once, now plotted against each other across every threshold.

ROC-AUC

Area under the ROC curve

0.5 = no better than random ranking; 1.0 = a perfect ranking that separates every positive above every negative.

PR-AUC

Area under the PR curve

Baseline is the dataset's own positive rate (e.g. 22.3% for CFPB), not 0.5 — this is the key asymmetry Section 09 makes precise.

07 · COMMON MISTAKES

What beginners often misunderstand

"ROC-AUC of 0.9 always means a strong model."

Under heavy class imbalance, ROC-AUC can stay high even while precision at any usable threshold is poor — Section 09 shows the mechanism.

"PR-AUC and ROC-AUC share the same 0.5 baseline."

PR-AUC's random-model baseline is the dataset's own positive rate, not 0.5 — a rare-event problem's PR-AUC "floor" can be far below 0.5.

"This replaces Concept 03's precision and recall."

No — a deployed model still operates at one chosen threshold. This concept helps choose that threshold and compare models; Concept 03's ratios are still what gets reported for the one threshold actually shipped.

"More threshold points always means a smoother, more accurate curve."

With only 10-11 points here the curves are jagged by construction — real systems use finer-grained scores, not more theory.

04
INTUITION

A metal detector's sensitivity dial

A metal detector with an adjustable sensitivity dial can be turned up to catch every scrap of metal (high recall, many false alarms on belt buckles and coins) or turned down to only alert on large objects (high precision, but it misses smaller threats). Sweeping the dial from minimum to maximum and plotting hit-rate against false-alarm-rate at every setting is exactly the ROC curve; plotting precision against hit-rate at every setting is exactly the PR curve. Neither curve requires committing to one dial setting — that choice comes later.

Analogy boundaryA physical dial moves continuously; Model D's sweep here only has 11 discrete stops (k=0 to k=10) because the toy dataset only has 10 rows. A production model's continuous scores would trace a smoother version of the same two curves — the mechanics, not the smoothness, are what this concept teaches.

09
MATHEMATICS

Two independent ways to compute each AUC

ROC-AUC, method 1 (trapezoidal): integrate the (FPR, TPR) points using the trapezoid rule across all 11 thresholds. ROC-AUC, method 2 (rank-based): count, for every (relief, non-relief) pair, whether the relief complaint's score is higher — ROC-AUC = (# pairs where relief scores higher) / (# total pairs), a classical equivalence with the Mann-Whitney U statistic. For Model D, 3 relief × 7 non-relief = 21 pairs; 17 of them have the relief score ranked higher, giving 17/21 ≈ 0.810 by both methods.

ROC-AUC = (# concordant relief/non-relief pairs) / (# total pairs) = 17/21 ≈ 0.810

PR-AUC, method 1 (precision-at-rank sum, sklearn's average-precision formula): at each of the 3 relief rows' own rank position, record precision at that exact point, then average: relief rows appear at ranks 1, 3, and 6 → precision there is 1/1, 2/3, 3/6 → average = (1 + 0.667 + 0.5)/3 ≈ 0.722. PR-AUC, method 2 (trapezoidal on the 11-point PR curve) gives the same value by construction, since both are standard equivalent formulations.

PR-AUC (average precision) = mean of precision-at-each-relief-row's-rank = (1/1 + 2/3 + 3/6) / 3 = 13/18 ≈ 0.722

Both AUC values were independently cross-checked by two distinct methods before being used anywhere in this lesson — 17/21 and 13/18 are exact fractions, not rounded approximations.

CONCRETE USE

Examples and counterexamples

THIS COURSE'S PROJECT

Model D at CFPB's real 22.3% relief rate

ROC-AUC ≈ 0.810 looks solid; PR-AUC ≈ 0.722 against a 30% (here) or 22.3% (real CFPB) baseline tells a more scale-appropriate story.

BOUNDARY CASE

A perfectly balanced 50/50 dataset

Here ROC-AUC and PR-AUC tend to track each other much more closely — the divergence Section 09 highlights is specifically an imbalance phenomenon.

COUNTEREXAMPLE

"High ROC-AUC always means deploy it"

A model can have ROC-AUC 0.85 on a 1%-positive-rate problem and still have unusably low precision at every threshold that catches a meaningful fraction of positives.

MISTAKE TO AVOID

Comparing ROC-AUC across datasets with different imbalance levels

ROC-AUC's insensitivity to class balance makes it a poor cross-dataset comparison tool; PR-AUC is more sensitive to exactly this difference.

CODE

Both curves and both AUCs, from scratch

PYTHON · FROM-SCRATCH ROC-AUC AND PR-AUC (RANK-BASED) 
import numpy as np

scores = np.array([0.95, 0.88, 0.81, 0.72, 0.63, 0.55, 0.44, 0.31, 0.22, 0.10])
labels = np.array([1,    0,    1,    0,    0,    1,    0,    0,    0,    0])

def roc_auc_rank(scores, labels):
    pos = scores[labels == 1]
    neg = scores[labels == 0]
    wins = sum((p > n) + 0.5 * (p == n) for p in pos for n in neg)
    return wins / (len(pos) * len(neg))

def average_precision(scores, labels):
    order = np.argsort(-scores)
    labels_sorted = labels[order]
    hits, total_precision = 0, 0.0
    for i, y in enumerate(labels_sorted, start=1):
        if y == 1:
            hits += 1
            total_precision += hits / i
    return total_precision / labels.sum()

print(f"ROC-AUC = {roc_auc_rank(scores, labels):.3f}")   # 0.810
print(f"PR-AUC (avg precision) = {average_precision(scores, labels):.3f}")  # 0.722

assert round(roc_auc_rank(scores, labels), 3) == round(17/21, 3)
assert round(average_precision(scores, labels), 3) == round(13/18, 3)

ECOSYSTEM

The library versions

sklearn.metrics.roc_auc_score and sklearn.metrics.average_precision_score compute exactly Section 09's two rank-based formulas given true labels and continuous scores — no manual threshold sweep required. Saito & Rehmsmeier (2015) make the empirical case this concept's Section 08/18 rely on: under class imbalance, ROC curves can look deceptively similar across models whose PR curves clearly differ, arguing PR curves are the more informative choice specifically for imbalanced classification.

Failure modes

ROC-ONLY REPORTING

Hides exactly the precision weakness PR-AUC would expose.

Comparing PR-AUC to 0.5 like ROC-AUC

The same model architecture can show very different ROC-AUC purely from a shift in class balance, unrelated to model quality.

Shipping "the AUC" instead of a chosen threshold

AUC summarizes the sweep; production still needs one committed threshold, Concept 07's job.

18
REALITY

Production implications

  • Report PR-AUC alongside ROC-AUC for any imbalanced problem, and state the positive rate explicitly so PR-AUC's baseline is legible to readers.
  • Never compare ROC-AUC across model versions evaluated on differently-imbalanced test splits without accounting for that difference first.
  • Log both AUCs per model version in the registry (Module 18) alongside whichever single threshold gets deployed, so future comparisons aren't threshold-dependent.

RECALL

Knowledge check

What are the two axes of the ROC curve, and of the PR curve?
ROC: FPR (x) vs. TPR (y). PR: recall (x) vs. precision (y).
Why is 0.5 the correct random-model baseline for ROC-AUC but not for PR-AUC?
ROC-AUC's rank-based interpretation (probability a random positive outranks a random negative) is exactly 0.5 for random scores regardless of class balance; PR-AUC's baseline is precision at random ranking, which equals the dataset's own positive rate.
Compute ROC-AUC's rank-based formula for a dataset with 2 positives and 3 negatives where every positive outranks every negative.
6/6 = 1.0 — a perfect ranking.

21
FEEDBACK

Solution and teach-back

Open the worked solution
ROC-AUC: of the 3×3=9 positive/negative pairs, count how many have the positive ranked higher. Positives sit at ranks 1, 2, 4; negatives at ranks 3, 5, 6. Rank-1 positive beats all 3 negatives (3 wins); rank-2 positive beats all 3 (3 wins); rank-4 positive beats ranks 5 and 6 only (2 wins) = 8/9 ≈ 0.889. PR-AUC: positives appear at ranks 1, 2, 4 with precision 1/1, 2/2, 3/4 → average = (1 + 1 + 0.75)/3 ≈ 0.917. On a dataset with far fewer positives than negatives, PR-AUC tends to diverge more sharply from ROC-AUC, because a fixed number of false positives eats a much larger share of a small positive-predicted pile than of a large one — exactly Section 09's imbalance mechanism.

Teach-back: explain in your own words why this 6-row example's ROC-AUC and PR-AUC are close together, while Model D's (0.810 vs. 0.722) are further apart — connect it to each dataset's actual class balance.

23
CAREER PRACTICE

Interview questions

Why would you prefer PR-AUC over ROC-AUC for a rare-event classification problem?
PR-AUC's baseline scales with the positive rate, so it stays sensitive to precision weakness that ROC-AUC's 0.5-baseline can mask under heavy imbalance — Saito & Rehmsmeier's empirical argument.
Explain the rank-based interpretation of ROC-AUC in one sentence.
It's the probability that a randomly chosen positive example is ranked above a randomly chosen negative example by the model's scores.
Coding: how would you compute PR-AUC from raw scores and labels without a library?
Sort by score descending, walk down the list tracking a running count of true positives seen so far, and average the precision (running TP / rank position) at each row where a true positive occurs — exactly Section 12's average_precision function.
Debugging: a colleague reports ROC-AUC 0.88 and is confident the model is ready to ship. What do you ask for next?
The PR-AUC and the actual class balance of the evaluation set — a high ROC-AUC alone doesn't rule out unusable precision at any realistic deployment threshold under imbalance.
