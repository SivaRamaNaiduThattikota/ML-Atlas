MODULE 02 · CONCEPT 08

A metric that was right
can still get gamed.

Concept 07 chose which of Concepts 01-06's six metrics should lead, guard and watch a given business problem — a careful, defensible choice. This concept is the warning that follows any such choice: a metric is always a proxy for the real goal it stands in for, never the goal itself. Goodhart's law — "when a measure becomes a target, it ceases to be a good measure" — says that pushing a proxy hard enough, with no guardrail, can make the proxy climb while the real goal it was chosen to protect quietly falls.

01 · CONCEPT FIRST

Goodhart's law: "when a measure becomes a target, it ceases to be a good measure"

THE PROXY

Whatever number a metric actually reports — Concept 07's chosen tuning metric, for instance. Easy to observe, easy to optimize directly.

THE REAL GOAL

Whatever the metric was originally chosen to stand in for — the actual business outcome, which is often harder to measure directly than the proxy itself.

First memory: a metric is a stand-in, not the thing itself — and a stand-in can be pushed up in ways that leave the real thing unchanged, or worse.
02 · WHY IT EXISTS

Choosing the right metric (Concept 07) doesn't make it ungameable

Concept 07's five-question filter picks the metric that best represents a business problem right now. It says nothing about what happens once people or systems start optimizing that exact number as hard as they can. If there is any way to raise the proxy that does not require raising the real goal — a "gaming channel" — enough optimization pressure will eventually find it, whether or not anyone intends to cheat.

WITHOUT THIS CONCEPT

A team sees their chosen metric climbing quarter over quarter and assumes the real goal is improving in step with it.

WITH THIS CONCEPT

A team asks: is there a way to move this number that doesn't require the real goal to move too? If yes, that gap needs a guardrail before it's trusted.

03 · CONCRETE EXAMPLE

CFPB's complaint-closure rate, under rising pressure to hit an SLA

Say CFPB starts tracking a proxy metric — the percentage of complaints closed within a service-level target — alongside the real goal, decision correctness (did the closure actually reach the right outcome for the complainant?). As pressure to hit the SLA rises from none to heavy, with no guardrail in place:

NO PRESSURE

Closure rate 60%, decision correctness 80% — both numbers describe a system with no incentive to cut corners yet.

MODERATE PRESSURE

Closure rate 69%, correctness 86% — genuinely both improving together; some real efficiency gains are still available.

HEAVY PRESSURE, NO GUARDRAIL

Closure rate 90%, correctness 55% — the proxy kept climbing the whole time, but correctness peaked early and then fell well below where it started.

The team that only watches the closure-rate proxy sees uninterrupted good news. The team that also watches correctness sees the real goal quietly get worse while the reported number looks better than ever.

04 · TECHNICAL MECHANISM

Four conditions, in sequence, that decide whether a metric decouples

A metric gets chosen — Concept 07's process, for good reasons, given the problem as it stood.
Real optimization pressure gets applied — a bonus, an SLA, a leaderboard, a promotion tied to the number.
Does a gaming channel exist — some way to raise the proxy that does not require raising the real goal?
If yes, does a guardrail exist — an audit, a floor on a second metric, something checking that the gains are real?
No guardrail — the proxy and the real goal decouple as pressure keeps rising (b03's heavy-pressure row).
Guardrail present — the gaming channel gets closed off or capped, and the coupling holds even under pressure.
05 · ARCHITECTURE / FLOW

From a careful choice to a warning about pushing it too hard

Concept 07 answered "which metric?" This concept answers "what happens to that metric under real pressure?" Goodhart's law is about a real move in the wrong direction; Concept 09 is about moves that were never real in the first place.

06 · IMPORTANT DISTINCTIONS

Gaming, genuine improvement, and measurement error — three different reasons a proxy moves

CauseWhat's actually happeningCommon confusion
Genuine improvementThe proxy rises because the real goal actually improvedAssumed to be the only possible explanation for a rising number — often true early, not guaranteed to stay true
Gaming (Goodhart's law)The proxy rises through a channel that doesn't touch the real goal at all — this concept's subjectAssumed to require bad faith — it can emerge purely from incentive pressure, with no one intending to cheat
Measurement errorThe proxy rises because of a flaw in how it's computed, not because of any real behavior changeConflated with gaming — a measurement bug is a data-quality problem, not an optimization-pressure problem, even though both can produce a misleading rising number
07 · COMMON MISTAKES

What beginners often misunderstand

"Goodhart's law means metrics can't be trusted at all."

Overcorrection — the fix is a guardrail on the gaming channel, not abandoning metrics; b04's guarded scenario still uses the proxy, just with a check attached.

"This only happens when people act in bad faith."

False — b03's example needs no dishonesty, only pressure and an unguarded gaming channel; the effect emerges from incentives, not intent.

"If the real goal isn't being measured directly, there's nothing to do about it."

A guardrail doesn't require measuring the real goal continuously — an occasional audit sample, checked against the proxy's claims, is often enough (b08).

"A metric that decoupled once will always decouple."

b04's guarded scenario shows the same proxy, under the same pressure, staying coupled once a guardrail closes the specific gaming channel that caused the problem.

THE GUARDRAILAn audit sample checking closure correctness

Periodically re-reviewing a random sample of "closed" complaints for actual correctness closes the gaming channel — exactly the kind of guardrail metric Concept 07's framework already has a slot for.

That's an operational tuning question, not this concept's job — the point here is that some guardrail is needed, not its precise calibration.

ORIENTATION

The warning that follows any metric choice

Concepts 01-07 built and then chose among six metrics for a stated business problem. This concept is that follow-up: any proxy metric — however well chosen — can decouple from the real goal it represents once optimization pressure meets an unguarded gaming channel. 04
INTUITION

A thermometer nailed to the wall next to the fireplace

A thermometer is a proxy for room temperature. Nail it to the wall right next to a fireplace, and someone trying to "raise the reported temperature" can just light the fire nearby — the thermometer climbs, and the rest of the room stays exactly as cold as before. The thermometer was a perfectly good proxy for room temperature right up until someone had a reason to specifically optimize the thermometer's reading rather than the room's actual warmth — at which point the gap between the two was always there, just never exploited.

Analogy boundaryThe fireplace illustrates "a channel that moves the proxy without moving the real thing," not the exact arithmetic — b03's actual numbers come from the illustrative simulation in Section 12, not from this analogy.

PRECISION

Formal definitions

TermDefinitionBoundary
Proxy metricAn observable, optimizable number chosen (Concept 07) to stand in for a real goal that is often harder to measure directlyNever identical to the real goal — only correlated with it, and only within some range of behavior
Gaming channelAny action that raises the proxy metric without requiring the real goal to improveCan exist with or without anyone intending to exploit it — its mere existence is the risk factor, not intent
Guardrail (reused from Concept 07)A floor, ceiling, or audit check the optimization is not allowed to violateDoes not need to measure the real goal continuously — a periodic sample often suffices (Section 18)
Goodhart's lawOnce a measure becomes the direct target of optimization, its correlation with the real goal it was chosen to represent tends to break down — precisely where a gaming channel exists and no guardrail closes itNot a claim that all metrics are useless — a metric with no gaming channel, or one with a working guardrail, does not exhibit this failure
06
MECHANISM

Tracing the four-step check on CFPB's example

Step 1 — metric chosen: closure-rate SLA, a reasonable proxy for handling complaints promptly (Concept 07's process, applied earlier).
Step 2 — pressure applied: reviewers' performance reviews tie directly to the SLA number.
Step 3 — gaming channel check: yes — closing a complaint faster does not require closing it correctly.
Step 4 — guardrail check, unguarded case: none — nothing checks whether closures were actually correct.
Result, unguarded: closure rate climbs to 90%, correctness falls to 55% (b03).
Step 4 — guardrail check, audited case: an audit sample catches and disqualifies gamed closures.
Result, audited: closure rate caps near 68%, correctness keeps rising to 88% (b04's figure).

09
MATHEMATICS

The optimization-pressure argument, formalized

Optimization pressure p pushes actions toward argmax_a m(a), where m is the proxy metric. If a subset of actions G exists where raising m(a) does not require raising the real goal g(a) — a gaming channel — then as p grows, an unconstrained search increasingly favors actions drawn from G, since they are typically the cheapest way to raise m. A guardrail works by excluding or penalizing G directly, so the search is forced back toward actions that raise m and g together.

argmax_a m(a), unconstrained → increasingly drawn from G as pressure p grows, whenever G exists and is cheaper than raising g directly argmax_a m(a), subject to a ∈ (action space \ G) → forced to raise m only via actions that also raise g

BOUNDARY CASE

A metric with no gaming channel at all

CODE

The pressure-sweep tables, from scratch

PYTHON · ILLUSTRATIVE SIMULATION, NOT REAL CFPB DATA 
# Section 10's two scenarios -- an illustrative simulation used to teach the
# shape of Goodhart's law, not a fit to real CFPB numbers. Pressure runs 0-10.
proxy_ungated    = [60,63,66,69,72,75,78,81,84,87,90]
true_goal_ungated = [80,83,85,86,85,82,78,73,67,61,55]
proxy_audited    = [60,62,64,65,66,67,67,68,68,68,68]
true_goal_audited = [80,82,84,85,86,87,87,88,88,88,88]

def is_monotonic_nondecreasing(xs):
    return all(xs[i] >= xs[i-1] for i in range(1, len(xs)))

def peak_index(xs):
    return max(range(len(xs)), key=lambda i: xs[i])

assert is_monotonic_nondecreasing(proxy_ungated)            # proxy always climbs
assert is_monotonic_nondecreasing(proxy_audited)            # audited proxy climbs too, just less
assert is_monotonic_nondecreasing(true_goal_audited)        # audited real goal never falls
p = peak_index(true_goal_ungated)
assert p == 3 and true_goal_ungated[p] == 86                # unguarded real goal peaks, then falls
assert true_goal_ungated[-1] < true_goal_ungated[0]         # ends below where it started
assert true_goal_audited[-1] > true_goal_ungated[-1]        # audited ends better than unguarded

ECOSYSTEM

Where this shows up beyond one project

Goodhart's law is documented across economics, education policy, and machine learning under closely related names — "reward hacking" and "specification gaming" in the reinforcement-learning literature describe an agent finding exactly this kind of gaming channel in its own reward function. Google's own "Rules of Machine Learning" guidance names the same failure directly: launch decisions should track long-term product goals, not just whichever proxy metric is easiest to move short-term (Section 24).

16
DIAGNOSIS

Failure modes

UNMONITORED PROXY

Tracking only the proxy, never the real goal

b03's unguarded scenario is invisible to a dashboard that only shows the closure-rate number — the real-goal collapse has no alarm attached.

GUARDRAIL THAT NOBODY CHECKS

An audit process that exists on paper but isn't actually reviewed

A guardrail only works if its results change behavior — an ignored audit report behaves identically to having no guardrail at all.

OVER-TIGHT GUARDRAIL

An audit so strict it blocks genuine improvement too

The audited scenario's lower proxy ceiling (68% vs. 90%) is the cost of safety — a guardrail calibrated too aggressively can suppress real gains along with gamed ones.

MOVING TARGET

Assuming a channel closed once stays closed

A new gaming channel can open later even after an old one is guarded against — the four-step check (b04) needs re-running whenever conditions change, not just once.

17
CORRECTION

Misconceptions

Incorrect beliefCorrection
A rising metric always means the real goal is improvingFalse — b03's unguarded scenario shows the proxy rising the entire time while the real goal falls well below its starting point.
Goodhart's law requires someone to act in bad faithFalse — it emerges from pressure meeting an unguarded gaming channel; no dishonest intent is required anywhere in the mechanism.
The fix is to stop using metrics altogetherFalse — the fix is a guardrail closing the specific gaming channel, keeping the proxy Concept 07 already chose for good reasons.
18
REALITY

Production implications

  • Whenever a metric becomes tied to a bonus, a review, or a leaderboard, explicitly re-run the four-step check (b04) — pressure is exactly the ingredient that turns a dormant gaming channel into a real risk.
  • Prefer a periodic audit sample over continuous real-goal measurement when the real goal is expensive to measure directly — Section 10's audited scenario needed only enough sampling to catch and disqualify gamed cases, not full coverage.
  • Re-check guardrails whenever the underlying process changes — a gaming channel that didn't exist when a guardrail was designed can open later, and the guardrail won't automatically cover it (Section 16's "moving target" failure).
19
RECALL

Knowledge check

State Goodhart's law in your own words, and name the two conditions this concept says decide whether it actually happens.
"When a measure becomes a target, it ceases to be a good measure" — decoupling happens specifically when a gaming channel exists AND no guardrail closes it (Section 09).
Why doesn't Goodhart's law require anyone to act dishonestly?
The mechanism is driven by optimization pressure finding the cheapest way to raise the proxy — if that cheapest way happens to be a gaming channel, pressure alone routes toward it, with no intent required (Section 04, Section 09).
Why is a guardrail usually the right fix, rather than abandoning the metric?
The metric was already the best available choice for the business problem (Concept 07) — a guardrail closes the specific gaming channel without discarding a proxy that is otherwise genuinely useful (Section 15).

21
FEEDBACK

Solution and teach-back

Open one defensible answer
Gaming channel: yes — an agent can send a fast, low-effort "we're looking into it" reply immediately, which counts as a response and lowers average response time, without resolving anything or actually helping the customer. Guardrail: track a second metric alongside average response time — resolution rate on first response, or a customer-satisfaction score sampled after ticket closure. A team gaming response time by sending empty replies would show a falling first-response-resolution rate even as their headline number improved, exactly the kind of divergence Section 10's table illustrates.

Teach-back: explain, in your own words, why "average response time" alone was a reasonable metric before the bonus was attached, and why attaching the bonus is exactly the moment Goodhart's law becomes a real risk rather than a hypothetical one.

PORTABLE MEMORY

Concept card

Metric-target misalignment, defined

Goodhart's law: pushing a chosen proxy metric hard enough, through an unguarded gaming channel, can decouple it from the real goal it was meant to represent.

Warning signs and example

Warning sign: a metric tied to a bonus, review, or leaderboard with no second check on it. Memorable example: CFPB's closure-rate SLA climbing from 60% to 90% while decision correctness quietly fell from 80% to 55%, with no guardrail in place.

23
CAREER PRACTICE

Interview questions

A stakeholder says "our metric has been climbing for six months, everything must be going well." What would you check before agreeing?
Whether a gaming channel exists for that specific metric, and whether any guardrail is actually being reviewed — a climbing proxy with no companion check is exactly the pattern Section 10's unguarded scenario describes, indistinguishable from genuine improvement without a second signal.
Coding: given two time series (a proxy metric and a real-goal metric) over rising pressure levels, how would you detect the kind of decoupling this concept describes?
Check whether the proxy series is monotonically non-decreasing while the real-goal series has an early peak followed by a sustained decline below its starting value — exactly Section 12's peak_index and monotonicity checks applied to real data instead of the illustrative simulation.
Debugging: a team's chosen metric (Concept 07's process) is rising, but a business stakeholder reports the real outcome feels worse. What's your first move?
Run the four-step check (b04): confirm a gaming channel actually exists, then check whether any guardrail is currently in place and being reviewed — the stakeholder's report is the real-goal signal the proxy alone can't provide.
System design: design a guardrail for a metric where the real goal is too expensive to measure on every case.
A periodic audit sample — re-check a random subset of cases against the real goal on a fixed cadence, exactly Section 10's audited scenario, which needed only enough sampling to catch and disqualify gamed cases, not full coverage.
