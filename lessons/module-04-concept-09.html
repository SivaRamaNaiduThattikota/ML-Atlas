01 · CONCEPT FIRST

CONCEPT 08 (usefulness)

THIS CONCEPT (the gap decision)

Given a feature that's already been kept, and whose gaps already have a diagnosed mechanism, what do you actually do about the missing cells? Fit-on-train imputation, a separate missingness indicator R, or a dedicated "missing" category.

First memory: Concept 01's three-part gate named "missing values" as something it feeds into. This is that handoff, resolved -- three concrete options, each paired to a mechanism Module 03 Concept 03 already diagnosed.
02 · WHY IT EXISTS

The obvious fix -- drop the row -- doesn't survive contact with production

The naive move is listwise deletion: drop any row with a gap. It throws away rows you already paid to collect, and worse, it can silently bias whatever's left if the mechanism isn't MCAR. But the real, non-negotiable failure is simpler: at prediction time, a live incoming complaint with a missing satisfaction_score still needs a prediction. There's no target row to drop. Dropping is a training-time convenience that doesn't exist as an option once the model is serving real traffic.

07 · COMMON MISTAKES

What beginners often assume, and shouldn't

"Dropping rows with gaps is always the safe default."

It fails outright at prediction time -- a live row with a missing field still needs a prediction, and there's no target row to drop from a served request.

"Mean imputation is fine no matter what caused the gap."

It's really only license under MCAR. Here, under a MAR mechanism, the mean-filled column reaches only |r|=0.4667 with the target -- the indicator reaches |r|=0.7454 on the exact same rows.

"The indicator is redundant once you've imputed the value."

Directly refuted by this concept's own numbers -- once every missing row gets the same fill, the fill can no longer tell those rows apart; R is where that difference actually survives.

06
MECHANISM

Five steps from diagnosis to a usable column

  1. Confirm the mechanism already diagnosed in EDA (Module 03 Concept 03) -- MCAR, MAR, or MNAR.
  2. Choose the option(s) that pair with that mechanism -- imputation alone can be enough under MCAR; MAR/MNAR usually need the indicator too.
  3. Fit the imputation statistic on TRAINING non-missing values only.
  4. Apply that same fitted value everywhere downstream -- training, holdout, and live traffic alike -- without ever refitting.
  5. Build R as a separate column regardless of whether imputation also happens -- it's cheap, fully observed, and the only place a flat fill's lost variation can still show up.

09
MATHEMATICS

The fill statistic, and why a constant can't carry variance

All three fill statistics are computed over TRAINING non-missing values only, exactly like Concept 04's scalers:

mean = (Σ x_i) / n
median = middle value of the sorted training non-missing values (average of the two middle values if n is even)
mode = most frequent training non-missing value

Once every missing row is filled with the same constant, that column can no longer distinguish those rows from each other -- whatever within-group variation existed among the missing rows' true values is gone from the imputed column itself. This is stated as a plain argument, not a strict inequality: it's why R has to carry what the constant fill structurally cannot.

10
WORKED EXAMPLE

The same 12 rows, three splits, four strategies

Training non-missing satisfaction_score values (ids 1,2,3,4,5,8): 9, 8, 9, 7, 6, 5.

mean = (9+8+9+7+6+5) / 6 = 44 / 6 = 7.3333
median = sorted [5,6,7,8,9,9] → average of the two middle values (7, 8) = 7.5

This single train-fitted mean (7.3333) fills every gap everywhere, including the holdout rows -- holdout never gets its own recomputed mean, even though its own two observed values (8, 9) would give a different, wrong 8.5. R is 1 for rows 6, 7, 9, 11 and 0 for the other eight rows.

A deliberate mechanism choice, stated plainlyModule 03 Concept 03's own prose imagined satisfaction_score missingness as MNAR -- the least-satisfied customers skip the survey, so the missing value drives its own absence. This concept picks a MAR reading instead, on purpose: missingness here depends entirely on prior_contact_attempts, an OBSERVED column, which makes it the reading that's actually diagnosable and fixable from the data alone -- MNAR can't be verified from data by construction. A real field could plausibly have elements of both, which is exactly why the indicator is the safety net when MNAR can't be fully ruled out.

The headline: on the honest training split, the flat mean-imputed value only reaches |r|=0.4667 with the target, while the missingness indicator alone reaches |r|=0.7454. Once every missing row gets the same constant fill value, the imputed column can no longer tell those rows apart -- whatever distinguished them (here, high prior_contact_attempts, low relief) survives only in R. The drop-rows baseline's 0.7000 is deliberately close to the indicator's number to make a separate point: it looks almost as informative on this split, but it costs 2 of 8 training rows outright, and -- the real killer -- it structurally cannot be applied at prediction time, since a live row with a missing satisfaction_score still needs a prediction.

11
CONCRETE USE

A clean fit, a boundary case, a production use, and a mistake

CLEAN FIT

An MCAR field, plain mean impute

When missingness has nothing to do with any observed or unobserved value, a simple fit-on-train mean or median usually suffices.

BOUNDARY CASE

The MAR satisfaction_score case

The indicator is required here -- a filter-style flat fill alone would silently destroy the signal the missing rows carried.

PRODUCTION USE

A versioned imputer + indicator artifact pair

Both ship together, fitted once on training data, echoing Concepts 02-04's fitted-artifact theme.

MISTAKE TO AVOID

Mean-imputing a suspected-MNAR field with no indicator at all

If the value's own content plausibly drives its own absence, a flat fill with no flag can hide that entirely.

12
CODE

A from-scratch train-fit imputer and indicator builder

PYTHON · FIT ON TRAIN, APPLY EVERYWHERE, NO LIBRARY CALL 
def fit_train_mean(values):
    """values: training non-missing entries only. Returns a single
    number, fit once and reused everywhere -- Concept 04's fit-on-train
    discipline applied to a fill statistic instead of a scaler."""
    return sum(values) / len(values)

def fit_train_median(values):
    s = sorted(values)
    n = len(s)
    mid = n // 2
    return s[mid] if n % 2 else (s[mid - 1] + s[mid]) / 2

def apply_fill(rows, fill_value):
    """rows: list of values, None where missing. Never refits -- the
    same fill_value is used on train, holdout, and live traffic."""
    return [fill_value if v is None else v for v in rows]

def build_indicator(rows):
    """R: 1 if missing, 0 if present -- fully observed by construction."""
    return [1 if v is None else 0 for v in rows]

train_s = [9, 8, 9, 7, 6, None, None, 5]  # ids 1-8, None = missing
non_missing = [v for v in train_s if v is not None]

mean_fill = fit_train_mean(non_missing)
median_fill = fit_train_median(non_missing)
assert round(mean_fill, 4) == 7.3333
assert median_fill == 7.5

filled = apply_fill(train_s, mean_fill)
indicator = build_indicator(train_s)
assert indicator == [0, 0, 0, 0, 0, 1, 1, 0]

"MCAR tolerates simple imputation" is a numeric-field rule of thumb -- it can still lose to own category for a categorical field, even under MCAR. A small contact_channel example (phone/chat/unknown, 8 rows) makes the case: phone=3/8=37.5%, chat=2/8=25%, unknown=3/8=37.5%, where unknown marks complaints logged before a CRM migration started tracking channel -- a structural, not-random gap.

contact_channelCountShare
phone3 / 837.5%
chat2 / 825.0%
unknown3 / 837.5%

The mode is TIED between phone and unknown at 37.5% each: mode-imputing would need an arbitrary tie-break and would silently overwrite more than a third of the field with a guess. Treating unknown as its own legitimate category needs no imputation decision at all, and loses nothing.

16
DIAGNOSIS

Failure modes

FLAT-FILL SIGNAL LOSS

A constant fill erases within-group variation

|r|=0.4667 (mean-imputed) versus |r|=0.7454 (indicator) on the identical training rows -- the same information, one column that can carry it and one that structurally can't.

DROP-ROWS' PREDICTION-TIME IMPOSSIBILITY

Deletion only exists during training

A live row needing a prediction has no target column to drop from -- the option simply isn't available once the model is serving traffic.

DISCARDING R AS "EXTRA MISSING DATA"

Module 03 Concept 03's own INDICATOR-AS-NOISE mistake

"R is fully observed by definition -- throwing it away throws away real information about the row, not just about the missing field." Resolved here by actually using R as a feature.

18
REALITY

Production implications

  • A fitted imputer's fill value and the indicator-builder should ship as one versioned artifact pair, echoing Concepts 02-04's persistence theme -- not re-derived separately per model run.
  • A field that starts MCAR can drift toward MAR or MNAR over time as upstream systems change, needing periodic re-diagnosis -- named forward to Module 17's drift monitoring.
  • The imputation statistic and the indicator must both be computed from the SAME training fit -- recomputing either one on new data reopens the exact leakage risk Concept 04 already closed for scalers.
19
RECALL

Knowledge check

Why did the missingness indicator R beat the imputed value on the training split?
Every missing row got the same constant fill, so the imputed column can't distinguish those rows from each other any more -- whatever separated them (here, high prior_contact_attempts, low relief) survives only in R, a fully observed flag, not in the flattened value.
Why does dropping rows fail at prediction time even if it looks fine on training data?
A live incoming row with a missing field still needs a prediction -- there's no target row to drop from a served request. Deletion is a training-time convenience that has no serving-time equivalent.
Why can own-category beat imputation for a categorical field even under MCAR?
Imputation forces a single guessed category onto every gap, which is especially costly when the mode is ambiguous (the tied 37.5%/37.5% contact_channel case) or the gap is itself a legitimate, informative state -- own-category loses nothing and needs no guess at all.

FEEDBACK

Solution and teach-back

Open the worked solution
This is MNAR -- the missingness depends on the field's own would-be value (same-day resolution), not on some other observed column, which is exactly the pattern that can't be verified from data alone. A flat mean/median fill risks hiding that same-day cases are systematically different, so the indicator is the safer default here, mirroring the satisfaction_score boundary case. Unlike satisfaction_score's MAR reading, this one can't lean on an observed column to confirm the mechanism -- which is precisely why the indicator, not imputation alone, is the conservative choice when MNAR can't be ruled out.

23
CAREER PRACTICE

Interview questions

Conceptual: why isn't dropping rows with missing values ever a complete solution?
It's unavailable at prediction time -- a live row with a gap still needs a prediction, and it can silently bias what's left in training when the mechanism isn't MCAR.
Mathematical: why does a constant imputed value necessarily reduce the within-group variance among the rows it fills?
Filling every missing row with the same number collapses whatever spread existed among their true (unknown) values down to zero variance on that column -- any information that spread carried about the target has to show up elsewhere, which is exactly the role R plays.
Coding: implement a train-fit mean imputer that raises an error if called on data containing no non-missing values.
Filter to non-missing training values first; if the resulting list is empty, raise rather than silently returning NaN or zero -- an imputer with nothing to fit on should fail loudly, not produce a meaningless fill value.
Debugging: a teammate's holdout accuracy jumped after they let the imputer refit its mean on holdout data before filling holdout gaps. What's wrong?
That's leakage into the fill statistic itself -- the same fit-on-train discipline Concept 04 established for scalers applies here. The training-fitted mean (7.3333, not holdout's own 8.5) has to fill every split, or the evaluation is no longer honest.
System design: how would you ship an imputer and its indicator for a field whose mechanism might drift over time?
Version the fitted imputer and indicator-builder together as one artifact pair, monitor the field's missingness rate and its conditional-rate pattern against known observed columns (Module 17, drift monitoring), and re-diagnose the mechanism -- not just re-fit the statistic -- whenever that pattern shifts materially.
