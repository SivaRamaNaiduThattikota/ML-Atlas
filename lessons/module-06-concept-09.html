MODULE 06 · CONCEPT 09

One model. One normalization.
No combination step required.

Concept 08 asked the same binary question K or K(K-1)/2 times and combined the answers -- OvR's argmax, OvO's majority vote. This concept doesn't combine anything. One model produces K raw scores, one per class, and softmax normalizes all of them together -- p_k=exp(z_k)/sum_j exp(z_j) -- into a genuine probability distribution that sums to exactly 1 by construction. Multiclass cross-entropy, -log(p_true), scores that distribution against whichever label actually happened.

01 · CONCEPT FIRST

Raw scores, normalized once, into a real distribution

RAW SCORES (z)

One number per class, from one linear model each -- any sign, any scale, no probabilistic meaning on their own.

SOFTMAX PROBABILITIES (p)

Normalize all K raw scores together: p_k=exp(z_k)/sum_j exp(z_j) -- positive, and guaranteed to sum to exactly 1.

First memory: Concept 08 combined K or K(K-1)/2 already-binary classifiers after training. This concept needs no combination step at all -- one model, one normalization, done.
02 · WHY IT EXISTS

OvR and OvO combine; softmax doesn't need to

Concept 08's OvR trains K separate classifiers, and nothing guarantees their own score scales match each other -- Concept 08's own Section 05 cited this directly from Wikipedia. OvO trains K(K-1)/2 pairwise classifiers and counts votes. Neither produces one jointly-trained, properly comparable probability distribution over all K classes at once. Softmax needs no combination step: one model scores every class, then normalizes all K scores together into shares that sum to exactly 1.

04 · TECHNICAL MECHANISM

Softmax, cited from Wikipedia and scikit-learn directly

Softmax (Wikipedia, Softmax function): "converts a tuple of K real numbers into a probability distribution over K possible outcomes. It is a generalization of the logistic function to multiple dimensions, and is used in multinomial logistic regression." scikit-learn's own multinomial-case formula (scikit-learn, Linear models): p_hat_k(X_i) = exp(X_i W_k + W0_k) / sum_l exp(X_i W_l + W0_l). At x1=2, x2=1, the three raw scores are z=(0.5, 0.7, 1.1). Softmax turns those into p=(0.2473, 0.3021, 0.4506), summing to exactly 1.0000.

07 · COMMON MISTAKES

What beginners often assume, and shouldn't

"Softmax's probabilities are just OvR's scores, rescaled to sum to 1."

False. Softmax's probabilities come from one jointly-trained model, not K independently-trained ones -- rescaling OvR's own scores after the fact would not fix its scale-comparability problem.

"Softmax's argmax must differ from OvR's argmax."

False, not guaranteed either way. What changed is comparability -- a real probability distribution instead of arbitrary scores -- not necessarily the predicted class itself.

04
INTUITION

One scoreboard, normalized into shares of 100%

Think of the raw scores z as one scoreboard with K entries, one per class -- any sign, any magnitude. Softmax turns that scoreboard into shares of a whole: exponentiate every entry (making everything positive), then divide each by the total. Whatever came in, whatever came out sums to exactly 100%.

Analogy boundaryThe "shares of 100%" analogy explains the NORMALIZATION correctly. But the shares still depend entirely on the model's own raw scores -- softmax guarantees a valid distribution, not a well-calibrated one. Calibration in general is named forward to Module 16, not covered here.

06
MECHANISM

Two steps, and only two steps

STEP 1 -- SCORE EVERY CLASS ONCE

One jointly-trained model, K weight vectors: z_k=w0^(k)+w1^(k)x1+w2^(k)x2, one raw score per class, computed once.

STEP 2 -- NORMALIZE ALL K SCORES AT ONCE

p_k=exp(z_k)/sum_j exp(z_j) -- every class's probability depends on every OTHER class's score too, unlike Concept 08's own per-classifier scores which never interact.

Contrast with Concept 08: OvR's step 2 was argmax over independent scores; OvO's step 2 was majority vote over pairwise duels. This concept's step 2 is a single joint normalization -- no combination of separate classifiers at all.

09
MATHEMATICS

The sum-to-1 proof, and the K=2 collapse

Softmax: p_k=exp(z_k)/sum_{j=1}^{K} exp(z_j). Since exp(z_j)>0 for every real z_j, every p_k is strictly positive. Summing across all K classes:

sum_k p_k = sum_k [exp(z_k) / sum_j exp(z_j)] = [sum_k exp(z_k)] / [sum_j exp(z_j)] = 1

The numerator and denominator are the exact same sum, so the ratio is always exactly 1 -- regardless of what the raw scores z happen to be. This is a property of the FORMULA, not a coincidence that needs checking case by case.

At K=2, softmax collapses to Concept 04's own sigmoid. With z_0 and z_1:

p_1 = exp(z_1) / (exp(z_0) + exp(z_1)) = 1 / (1 + exp(-(z_1-z_0))) = sigma(z_1-z_0)

Dividing numerator and denominator by exp(z_1) reduces the K-class softmax formula exactly to Concept 04's own sigmoid function, applied to the score DIFFERENCE z_1-z_0 -- confirming softmax is the genuine generalization Wikipedia's own Multinomial logistic regression page describes, not a separate formula that merely resembles it.

z, p, the sum check, and the loss

Weights (fixed, assumed already fit -- not derived here). class 0: w=(0.1, 0.3, -0.2); class 1: w=(0.4, -0.1, 0.5); class 2: w=(-0.2, 0.6, 0.1). Test point: x1=2, x2=1 -- fresh, sharing no numbers with any of Concept 08's own weights or five test points.

Step 1 -- raw scores. z0=0.1+0.3(2)-0.2(1)=0.5; z1=0.4-0.1(2)+0.5(1)=0.7; z2=-0.2+0.6(2)+0.1(1)=1.1.

Step 2 -- softmax. exp(0.5)=1.64872, exp(0.7)=2.01375, exp(1.1)=3.00417; sum=6.66664. p=(0.24730917976320385, 0.3020641142811064, 0.45062670595568977), rounded to p=(0.2473, 0.3021, 0.4506). Sum = 1.0000000000 exactly.

Step 3 -- cross-entropy. For reference: if true label were class 0, CE=1.3971; if true label were class 2 (the argmax), CE=0.7971 -- loss falls as the stated true class's own probability rises, exactly as the formula predicts.

Maximum entropy: p=(1/3, 1/3, 1/3)

The most uncertain possible 3-class distribution -- CE=ln(3)≈1.0986 for ANY true label, since every class is assigned the exact same probability. A reference point, not a case this concept's own weights happen to produce.

CODE

Softmax and cross-entropy, implemented directly

PYTHON · SOFTMAX + MULTICLASS CROSS-ENTROPY 
import math

SOFTMAX_WEIGHTS = [(0.1, 0.3, -0.2), (0.4, -0.1, 0.5), (-0.2, 0.6, 0.1)]

def score(w, x1, x2):
    w0, w1, w2 = w
    return w0 + w1 * x1 + w2 * x2

def softmax(zs):
    exps = [math.exp(z) for z in zs]
    total = sum(exps)
    return [e / total for e in exps]

def cross_entropy(probs, true_label):
    return -math.log(probs[true_label])

zs = [score(w, 2, 1) for w in SOFTMAX_WEIGHTS]
probs = softmax(zs)
assert zs == [0.5, 0.7, 1.1]
assert round(sum(probs), 10) == 1.0
assert round(cross_entropy(probs, 1), 4) == 1.1971  # confidently wrong

zs_ex = [score(w, -2, 2) for w in SOFTMAX_WEIGHTS]
probs_ex = softmax(zs_ex)
assert round(cross_entropy(probs_ex, 1), 4) == 0.1336  # confidently right

16
DIAGNOSIS

Where softmax actually fails

MISCALIBRATION WHEN OVERCONFIDENT

A valid distribution is not a well-calibrated one

Softmax guarantees probabilities that sum to 1, not probabilities that match real-world frequencies. Modern classifiers are well known to become overconfident; fixes like temperature scaling and label smoothing are named forward, not built here.

NUMERICAL OVERFLOW WITHOUT THE MAX-SHIFT TRICK

exp(z) can overflow for large z

A raw, unshifted exp/sum-exp implementation can overflow for large scores. This lesson's own verification (Section 10) used a max-shifted, numerically-stabilized softmax alongside the raw version specifically to confirm they agree -- the standard production fix, not an optional detail.

REALITY

Production implications

  • scikit-learn's multinomial LogisticRegression and any neural network's softmax output head are the standard production path for genuinely multiclass problems -- one jointly-trained model instead of managing K or K(K-1)/2 separate estimators.
  • Per-class probability outputs enable downstream thresholding exactly the way Concept 05's own odds/threshold machinery worked for the binary case -- just applied per class instead of to a single score.
  • Softmax's own overconfidence risk (Section 16) is a real production concern for any dashboard reporting a single "confidence" number -- echoing Module 02's own calibration concept by name only, not re-covered here.

CAREER PRACTICE

Interview questions

Conceptual: why does softmax not need a combination step the way OvR and OvO both do?
Because softmax is one jointly-trained model producing all K scores at once, normalized together in a single step -- there are no separate classifiers to combine in the first place.
Mathematical: derive softmax's sum-to-1 property and show it reduces to Concept 04's sigmoid at K=2.
sum_k p_k=[sum_k exp(z_k)]/[sum_j exp(z_j)]=1 for any z. At K=2, p_1=exp(z_1)/(exp(z_0)+exp(z_1)); dividing numerator and denominator by exp(z_1) gives p_1=1/(1+exp(-(z_1-z_0)))=sigma(z_1-z_0), Concept 04's own sigmoid applied to the score difference.
Coding: why does a production softmax implementation subtract max(z) before exponentiating?
To prevent numerical overflow -- exp(z) can grow enormous for large raw scores, while exp(z-max(z)) is bounded by 1 and produces mathematically identical probabilities, since the max-shift cancels out in the ratio (Section 16's own failure note).
System design: contrast OvR, OvO and softmax for a real multiclass pipeline.
OvR (Concept 08) is a fair default for large K, needing only K classifiers but with a real per-classifier scale-comparability risk. OvO (Concept 08) needs K(K-1)/2 classifiers, useful specifically when the base estimator benefits from smaller, more balanced sub-problems. Softmax needs one jointly-trained model and produces a genuinely comparable, properly normalized probability distribution across all K classes at once -- the standard choice for neural networks and multinomial logistic regression alike.
