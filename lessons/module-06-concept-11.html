01 · CONCEPT FIRST

A promise check, not a family choice

CONCEPTS 01-09 / 10

Built the linear/logistic/LDA/softmax family's mechanics, then asked whether that whole family belonged in the running for a given dataset at all.

THIS CONCEPT

Asks whether an ALREADY-chosen linear model's own math promises -- linearity, independence, homoscedasticity, no perfect multicollinearity, shared covariance -- are actually true of the data in front of it.

First memory: a scale promises an accurate reading only if it's been zeroed and kept level. The promise has conditions. This concept is about noticing when those conditions aren't met -- not about whether to use a scale at all.
02 · WHY IT EXISTS

Two threads this module deferred, paid off here

Concept 02 fit OLS by minimizing squared error and named its own assumption set -- linearity, independence, homoscedasticity, normal errors -- without checking any of them against real data. Concept 07 compared LDA against QDA and named the "assumptions/violations trade-off" between them -- LDA's cheaper shared-covariance assumption versus QDA's more expensive per-class estimate -- without showing what a violation of that assumption actually costs. This concept pays off both threads. It is distinct from Concept 10: that concept asked whether to reach for the linear family at all; this one asks whether a model you've already chosen is honoring its own math.

03 · CONCRETE EXAMPLE

complexity_score and resolution_hours, eight rows

A fresh CFPB-style toy dataset: x=complexity_score, an ordinal complaint-complexity rating assigned at intake (1 to 8), and y=resolution_hours, the analyst hours actually spent resolving that complaint. Eight rows: (1,13), (2,12), (3,19), (4,14), (5,25), (6,16), (7,31), (8,18). Nothing here has been used by name in Concepts 01-10.

04 · TECHNICAL MECHANISM

Fit the line, then look at what's left over

OLS on all 8 rows (Concept 02's own closed-form formula, not re-derived here) gives resolution_hours ≈ 11.4286 + 1.5714 × complexity_score. The residuals -- what the line misses -- are, in complexity_score order: 0, -2.57, 2.86, -3.71, 5.71, -4.86, 8.57, -6.00. Split those eight residuals at the midpoint: the low half (complexity_score 1-4) has a much smaller spread than the high half (5-8). That growing spread, not any single residual, is the signal this concept is built around.

06 · IMPORTANT DISTINCTIONS

Two different families, two different promises

OLS's own 4 assumptionsLDA's own 1 assumption
  • Linearity
  • Independence
  • Homoscedasticity (constant error variance)
  • No perfect (or near-perfect) multicollinearity
  • Shared covariance across every class's Gaussian -- QDA drops this and pays a separate per-class covariance-estimation cost instead (Concept 07's own trade-off, cited back)

These are two different model families' own promises, not one universal checklist -- OLS's list comes from Section 05's Molnar citation; LDA's comes from Concept 07's own scikit-learn citation.

07 · COMMON MISTAKES

What beginners often assume, and shouldn't

"An assumption violation always means the model is useless."

False. OLS stays unbiased under heteroscedasticity -- it just becomes less precise, and its own reported standard errors and confidence intervals become misleading (Section 05's Wikipedia OLS quote). The coefficients themselves aren't wrong; trusting their reported uncertainty is what gets risky.

"A residual plot that isn't perfectly flat means something is badly wrong."

False. Real data always has some texture. The diagnostic signal here is a clear GROWING pattern -- the verified 6.35x ratio -- not any deviation from a perfectly flat line at all.

04
INTUITION

A scale that promises accuracy only under conditions

A scale reads accurately only if it has been zeroed and kept level -- the promise has conditions, and a reading taken without checking them can look perfectly normal while quietly being wrong. A linear model's own assumptions work the same way: OLS's coefficient table and confidence intervals are only as trustworthy as linearity, independence, homoscedasticity, and the absence of perfect multicollinearity actually holding for the data underneath them.

Imprecise is not brokenOLS stays unbiased under heteroscedasticity -- the fitted line itself is not wrong on average. What breaks is OLS's own reported uncertainty: its standard errors and the confidence intervals built from them become misleading, per the Wikipedia OLS quote in Section 05. That is a real problem, just a different one than "the model is broken."

09
MATHEMATICS

The variance-split ratio and the Goldfeld-Quandt F

Population variance of a residual set {e_1,...,e_n}: Var(e) = (1/n) × Σ(e_i - mean(e))². Splitting a fitted model's residuals into a low-x half and a high-x half and comparing Var(e_high) / Var(e_low) is the direct, informal version of this concept's own diagnostic. The Goldfeld-Quandt test formalizes it: split the sample by the suspected explanatory variable into a low subset and a high subset, fit SEPARATE OLS regressions on each, and use "the ratio of the mean square residual errors for the regressions on the two subsets" as an F-test statistic (Wikipedia, Goldfeld-Quandt test), compared against an F-distribution with (df_high, df_low) degrees of freedom.

Var(e) = (1/n) Σ (e_i - mean(e))²
F = MSE_high / MSE_low, df = (df_high, df_low)
MSE = SSE / df, from two SEPARATE subset regressions

WORKED EXAMPLE

6.35x by variance split, F=5.8 by Goldfeld-Quandt

Step 1 -- fit OLS on the full 8-row set. meanX=4.5, meanY=18.5, b1=Sxy/Sxx=11/7≈1.5714, b0=meanY-b1×meanX=80/7≈11.4286. resolution_hours ≈ 11.4286 + 1.5714 × complexity_score, SSE≈194.2857. Residuals in complexity_score order 1..8: 0, -2.5714, 2.8571, -3.7143, 5.7143, -4.8571, 8.5714, -6.0000.

Step 2 -- the informal diagnostic. LOW half (complexity_score 1-4) residuals: [0, -2.5714, 2.8571, -3.7143], population variance = 6.4082. HIGH half (complexity_score 5-8) residuals: [5.7143, -4.8571, 8.5714, -6.0000], population variance = 40.6939. Ratio HIGH/LOW = 6.3503 -- a concrete, computable signal that residual spread grows sharply with complexity_score.

Step 3 -- the named Goldfeld-Quandt test. Fit SEPARATE OLS lines on the LOW subset {(1,13),(2,12),(3,19),(4,14)} and HIGH subset {(5,25),(6,16),(7,31),(8,18)}. LOW fit: b0=12, b1=1, residuals [0,-2,4,-2], SSE=24, df=2, MSE=12. HIGH fit: b0=26.4, b1=-0.6, residuals [1.6,-6.8,8.8,-3.6], SSE=139.2, df=2, MSE=69.6. F = MSE_high/MSE_low = 5.8, with (2,2) degrees of freedom -- the exact statistic the Goldfeld-Quandt test defines.

CODE

Reproducing the split and the Goldfeld-Quandt F directly

PYTHON · OLS FIT, RESIDUAL SPLIT, AND GOLDFELD-QUANDT F 
X = [1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8]
Y = [13, 12, 19, 14, 25, 16, 31, 18]

def fit_ols(xs, ys):
    n = len(xs)
    mean_x = sum(xs) / n
    mean_y = sum(ys) / n
    sxy = sum((x - mean_x) * (y - mean_y) for x, y in zip(xs, ys))
    sxx = sum((x - mean_x) ** 2 for x in xs)
    b1 = sxy / sxx
    b0 = mean_y - b1 * mean_x
    return b0, b1

def residuals(xs, ys, b0, b1):
    return [y - (b0 + b1 * x) for x, y in zip(xs, ys)]

def pop_variance(values):
    mean = sum(values) / len(values)
    return sum((v - mean) ** 2 for v in values) / len(values)

# Full-set fit and the informal low/high residual-variance split.
b0, b1 = fit_ols(X, Y)
resid = residuals(X, Y, b0, b1)
low, high = resid[:4], resid[4:]
ratio = pop_variance(high) / pop_variance(low)
assert round(ratio, 4) == 6.3503

# Goldfeld-Quandt: two SEPARATE subset regressions.
b0_low, b1_low = fit_ols(X[:4], Y[:4])
b0_high, b1_high = fit_ols(X[4:], Y[4:])
sse_low = sum(r ** 2 for r in residuals(X[:4], Y[:4], b0_low, b1_low))
sse_high = sum(r ** 2 for r in residuals(X[4:], Y[4:], b0_high, b1_high))
mse_low, mse_high = sse_low / 2, sse_high / 2  # df=2 each
f_stat = mse_high / mse_low
assert round(f_stat, 1) == 5.8

Incorrect beliefCorrection

18
REALITY

Production implications

  • A deployed OLS or logistic baseline's coefficient table and confidence intervals are only as trustworthy as its own assumption set actually holding -- an assumption check is a cheap sanity pass before anyone downstream trusts a reported uncertainty number.
  • A periodic residual check -- the same fan-plot idea from Section 07, run on live production residuals -- is a low-cost way to catch a retrained model's reported uncertainty drifting away from reality.
  • Near-multicollinearity between engineered features (Module 04 Concept 06, cited back) is worth re-checking after any feature-engineering change, since new derived features can quietly reintroduce it.

RECALL

Knowledge check

Why does the residual-variance ratio come out to 6.3503x, and what does that number actually measure?
It is the population variance of the four high-complexity residuals (40.6939) divided by the population variance of the four low-complexity residuals (6.4082), both taken from the SAME full-set OLS fit. A ratio well above 1 means the model's errors are not spread evenly across the range of x -- they are systematically wider where complexity_score is higher.
Why is a Goldfeld-Quandt F=5.8 at df=(2,2) not a formal significance claim here?
The conventional F-critical threshold at that degrees-of-freedom pair and alpha=0.05 is roughly 19.0, well above 5.8. With only 4 rows per subset, this toy sample is too small to clear it -- the worked example demonstrates the test's mechanism and shows a real growing-variance pattern, but does not claim statistical significance on 8 rows.
Why is this concept's question different from Concept 10's, even though both are about linear models?
Concept 10 asked whether the linear/logistic/LDA/softmax family belonged in the running at all, BEFORE choosing and fitting a model. This concept asks whether a model ALREADY chosen and fit is honoring its own math assumptions -- a check made AFTER.

CAREER PRACTICE

Interview questions

Conceptual: name OLS's own 4 assumptions.
Linearity, independence, homoscedasticity, and the absence of perfect (or near-perfect) multicollinearity -- Molnar's own list also names normality and fixed features, but this concept's own worked example centers on the first four.
Mathematical: why doesn't heteroscedasticity bias OLS, but it does bias its standard errors?
OLS's coefficient estimates stay unbiased under heteroscedasticity because the estimator itself only requires the errors to average to zero, not to share the same variance. What breaks is the FORMULA used to estimate standard errors, which assumes constant error variance -- when that assumption fails, the reported standard errors (and the confidence intervals and p-values built from them) become misleading, per the Wikipedia OLS quote.
Mathematical: state the Goldfeld-Quandt mechanism.
Split the sample by the suspected explanatory variable into a low subset and a high subset, fit separate OLS regressions on each, and compute F as the ratio of the two subsets' mean-square residuals (MSE_high / MSE_low), compared against an F-distribution at (df_high, df_low).
System design: distinguish this concept's question from Concept 10's.
Concept 10 asks whether the linear/logistic/LDA/softmax family belongs in the running at all, using structural separability and bias-variance criteria -- a decision made before choosing a model. This concept asks whether an ALREADY-chosen and fit model's own assumptions are holding against real data -- a decision made after, and a different kind of risk entirely.
