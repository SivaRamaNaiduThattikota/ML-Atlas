MODULE 07 · CONCEPT 06

Stop voting. Stop scoring likelihoods.
Pick the one line with the widest empty strip.

Concepts 01-02 predicted by counting votes among nearest neighbors. Concepts 03-05 predicted by multiplying feature likelihoods under Bayes' rule. A support vector machine does neither: it commits to exactly one separating hyperplane -- the one whose margin (the empty strip between it and the nearest point of each class) is as wide as possible -- and names the handful of training points actually touching that margin. This concept reuses Module 06 Concept 06's own signed-distance formula to build that hyperplane by hand on four points, hard-margin and linear only.

01 · CONCEPT FIRST

A third mechanism, not a fourth flavor of the same two

Concepts 01-02 predicted by counting votes among the k nearest neighbors -- no fitted parameters at all. Concepts 03-05 predicted by multiplying per-feature likelihoods under Bayes' rule. A support vector machine does neither: it solves an optimization problem that commits to ONE fixed separating line and can name exactly which training points hold that line in place.

First memory: SVM doesn't vote and doesn't score probabilities -- it maximizes the width of the empty strip around one chosen line.
02 · WHY IT EXISTS

Reusing Module 06's own formula

Module 06 Concept 06 derived the signed distance from a point to a linear boundary: z = b + w1·x1 + w2·x2, distance = z/||w||, ||w|| = √(w1²+w2²). That concept named "SVM margins" forward from this exact formula without building one. This concept is that payoff -- the margin is nothing but this same distance, applied to the two nearest points of each class.

03 · CONCRETE EXAMPLE

What a margin actually is

Draw two lines parallel to the boundary, each just touching the nearest point of one class. The empty strip between those two lines is the margin. A support vector machine's entire job is choosing the boundary that makes that strip as wide as possible, not just any line that happens to separate the classes.

04 · IMPORTANT DISTINCTIONS

The precondition this lesson stays inside

A single straight max-margin line only exists cleanly when the two classes can be separated by a straight line at all -- linear separability, cited from Module 06 Concept 06 (Wikipedia, Linear separability). This is the "hard margin" case. Curved boundaries (the kernel trick) and points allowed to sit on the wrong side (soft margins) are both named forward only, in Concepts 07 and 08.

05 · ARCHITECTURE / FLOW

Four points, related to a dataset you've already seen

Reuse Module 06 Concept 10's feature names -- x1=prior_contact_attempts, x2=case_age_days -- and diagonal spirit, but with fresh numbers: that dataset's own separator wasn't equidistant from both classes, so it can't demonstrate a margin. New set: Resolve (y=-1): A=(1,1), D=(0,0). Escalate (y=+1): B=(3,3), C=(5,2).

06 · TECHNICAL MECHANISM

The answer: w=(0.5,0.5), b=-2

The max-margin line is x1+x2=4. Checking each point's margin condition y·(w·x+b): A and B both land exactly on the boundary of the constraint (value 1.0, equality); D lands at 2.0 and C at 1.5 -- both strictly past it, safely inside their class's zone. The resulting margin width is 1.414214.

07 · CONCRETE EXAMPLE

Naming the support vectors

A and B are the support vectors -- the only two points actually touching the margin and constraining where it sits. D and C could each move closer to the boundary, right up to the margin, without changing the line at all. That's the entire intuition an SVM is built around.

08 · YOUR PROJECT MAPPING

This isn't a metaphor -- it's a real attribute

SCIKIT-LEARN STORES THIS LITERALLYA fitted SVC keeps support_vectors_, support_, and n_support_.

After training, those attributes hold exactly the coordinates, indices, and per-class counts of the points touching the margin -- here, that would be A and B, not D or C.

ONE NUMBER DECIDES EVERYTHINGy·(w·x+b) = 1 means "on the margin," >1 means "safely past it."

No vote is counted and no probability is multiplied -- a support vector machine's classification of any new point comes from this one signed quantity.

09 · WHAT YOU SHOULD UNDERSTAND

The takeaway

Margin = the empty strip's width

Maximizing it means minimizing ||w|| subject to every point satisfying y_i(w·x_i+b) ≥ 1.

Support vectors are the points that matter

Only the points sitting exactly on the margin (here, A and B) constrain the line; every other point is free to move without changing anything.

This lesson is hard-margin and linear only

No curved boundary, no tolerance for misclassified points -- both come later, named forward.

Teach-back questions

  1. Why is D, at margin-value 2.0, not a support vector even though it belongs to the correct class?
  2. What does it mean, geometrically, for a point to satisfy its margin condition with exact equality?
  3. Which scikit-learn SVC attribute would tell you, after fitting, exactly which training points were support vectors?

01
ORIENTATION

Module 07's third mechanism

Concepts 01-02 (K-nearest neighbors, distance metrics) predict by counting votes among neighbors -- no fitted parameters. Concepts 03-05 (Naive Bayes and its three variants) predict by multiplying per-feature likelihoods under Bayes' rule. Neither family fits this concept's mechanism: a support vector machine solves an optimization that commits to exactly one separating hyperplane and names the specific training points constraining it.

02
CITATION

The reused primitive: signed distance to a hyperplane

Module 06 Concept 06 derived z = b + w1·x1 + w2·x2 and distance = z/||w||, where ||w|| = √(w1²+w2²). That concept named "SVM margins" forward from this exact formula. Nothing here rederives distance-to-a-hyperplane -- it is cited and reused directly.

03
PRECONDITION

Linear separability, cited

Hard-margin SVM assumes the two classes can be separated by a straight hyperplane at all -- linear separability (Wikipedia, Linear separability), cited from Module 06 Concept 06 without rederivation. This lesson stays entirely inside that assumption.

04
MATHEMATICS

Formalizing the margin

maximize margin = minimize ||w|| subject to y_i(w·x_i + b) ≥ 1 for every training point i

Two parallel hyperplanes, one on each side of the boundary, each touching the nearest point of one class -- the margin is the perpendicular distance between them, equal to 2/||w||, so minimizing ||w|| maximizes it. This is scikit-learn's own framing of "the largest margin between the outermost points of each class" (scikit-learn, Support Vector Machines).

05
ARCHITECTURE

The dataset, and why it's new

Module 06 Concept 10's 6-point diagonal-band dataset used feature names x1=prior_contact_attempts, x2=case_age_days -- reused here in spirit, not in numbers. That dataset's own separating line, x1+x2=5, is a valid separator but is not max-margin for its 6 points: its nearest class-0 point sits at distance 1.414214 while its nearest class-1 point sits at distance 2.121320 -- unequal, so it cannot illustrate a margin. A fresh, hand-verifiable 4-point set is used instead: Resolve (y=-1): A=(1,1), D=(0,0). Escalate (y=+1): B=(3,3), C=(5,2).

06
TECHNICAL MECHANISM

Derivation logic

With exactly one support vector expected per class, the optimal w must point along the line connecting them: w = α(B−A) = α(2,2). Solving the two equality constraints w·A+b=−1 and w·B+b=+1 simultaneously gives α=0.25, so w=(0.5,0.5) and b=−2.

07
MATHEMATICS

Resulting geometry

Boundary: x1 + x2 = 4. ||w|| = 0.707107. Margin = 1/||w|| = 1.414214.

10
DIAGNOSIS

Check 2 · KKT / dual stationarity

Solving α independently from each component of w = α(B−A): from w1=0.5=α·2, α=0.250000; from w2=0.5=α·2, α=0.250000. Both components agree exactly, and α≥0 confirms dual feasibility -- a necessary condition the max-margin solution must satisfy.

11
DIAGNOSIS

Check 3 · convex-hull distance cross-check

Each class's convex hull here is just its own 2-point segment (A-D for Resolve, B-C for Escalate). Dense sampling of both segments (20,000+ points each) finds the minimum distance between the two hulls to be 2.828427 -- exactly dist(A,B), and exactly 2× the margin (1.414214). This matches the classic duality between max-margin width and minimum inter-hull distance.

12
DIAGNOSIS

Check 4 · brute-force angular sweep

Sweeping 2,000,000 candidate separating directions θ ∈ [0, π) and computing the best achievable margin for each finds a maximum of exactly 1.414214, occurring at θ=π/4 -- precisely w's own direction, since atan2(0.5, 0.5)=π/4. No other orientation of the separating line beats it.

sklearn's real attributes, confirmed live

Verified against scikit-learn's own SVC documentation (scikit-learn, SVC): a fitted classifier stores support_ (the indices of support vectors), support_vectors_ (their coordinates -- here, exactly A and B, never D or C), and n_support_ (the count per class). scikit-learn's SVM guide (scikit-learn, Support Vector Machines) states its own canonical example titles, "SVM: Maximum margin separating hyperplane" and "SVM Margins Example" -- naming the exact concept this lesson builds by hand.

MECHANICS

How the readout updates

Since w=(0,1) and b=0, f(x) reduces to the probe's own y-value, and ||w||=1 makes distance-to-boundary equal to |f(x)| directly. y·f(x) is compared against 1 with a small snap tolerance (0.05): within that band of exact equality, the verdict names the probe a support vector; above 1, safely non-support; below 1, a margin violation a hard-margin SVM could never allow. The same update function runs on every slider drag and every class-toggle click, plus once on page load.

CAREER PRACTICE

Interview questions

Why are D and C, both correctly classified, not support vectors?
Their margin-condition value (2.0 and 1.5) is strictly greater than 1 -- they sit safely inside their class's zone and could move closer to the boundary without changing the max-margin solution at all. Only points at exact equality (value 1) constrain the line.
What is the geometric relationship between minimizing ||w|| and maximizing the margin?
The margin equals 2/||w||, so the two objectives are the same problem stated two ways -- minimizing ||w|| directly maximizes the margin width.
After fitting an SVC, which attribute tells you exactly which training points were support vectors?
support_vectors_ holds their coordinates directly; support_ holds their indices into the training data; n_support_ holds the count per class.
Why does this lesson use a fresh 4-point dataset instead of Module 06 Concept 10's own 6-point set?
Concept 10's separating line, x1+x2=5, is a valid separator but not max-margin -- its nearest class-0 distance (1.414214) and nearest class-1 distance (2.121320) are unequal, so it cannot demonstrate an actual margin.
22
CORRECTION

Common misconception

"Support vectors" are not simply "the points closest to the boundary" in some vague sense.They are precisely the points whose margin condition holds with exact equality, y_i(w·x_i+b)=1 -- a specific, checkable condition, not a judgment call about which points "look close." A point can look visually near the boundary and still not be a support vector if its exact value is strictly greater than 1.
