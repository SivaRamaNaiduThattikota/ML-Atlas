MODULE 07 · CONCEPT 08

Fewer numbers isn't always the same story as easier to read.
Count both, on four models you already built.

This concept introduces no new mechanism. It steps back and asks two separate questions of the four models Module 07 has already built by hand: how many fitted numbers does each one actually need, and could a person read its decision rule off those numbers without running any code? KNN needs zero fitted numbers but never lets go of the raw data. Naive Bayes needs exactly 7. A linear SVM needs exactly 3. A kernel SVM's count depends on the data itself -- and this concept sweeps it by hand, from cheaper than Naive Bayes to worse than KNN, using nothing but sklearn's own documented attribute shapes.

Two questions, not one, and neither is accuracy

Every concept so far in this module answered "how do you predict?" This one asks two different questions of the same four already-built models: how many numbers does the fitted model need to describe itself, and could a person read its rule off those numbers directly?

01 · CONCEPT FIRST

Two questions, and neither is accuracy

Complexity, here, means: how many numbers does the fitted model actually need to keep, to make a prediction on a brand-new point? Interpretability, here, means: could a person write the model's decision rule as one readable expression, without enumerating the training data? These are properties of the fitted model itself -- not of how well it predicts, and (as this concept shows) they don't always move together.

First memory: "How many numbers?" and "can you read the rule?" are two different questions -- a model can score well on one and poorly on the other.
02 · WHY IT EXISTS

A checkpoint before Concept 09 asks "when does each model still win"

Concepts 01-02 predicted by voting among neighbors. Concepts 03-05 predicted by scoring Bayes'-rule likelihoods. Concept 06 predicted by margin-maximization. Three genuinely different mechanisms, three different answers to "what does the fitted model even consist of." Before asking which one wins in which situation (Concept 09), this concept counts what each one actually costs to keep around.

03 · CONCRETE EXAMPLE

The model that never trains

Scikit-learn's own neighbors guide describes KNN plainly: it "does not attempt to construct a general internal model, but simply stores instances of the training data" (scikit-learn, Nearest Neighbors). Zero numbers are fitted. But Concept 02's own 4-point toy set (query at origin, k=3) must be kept in full to predict anything at all -- 4 points × 2 coordinates = 8 raw numbers, forever.

04 · IMPORTANT DISTINCTIONS

Three things people lump together as "the model"

Fitted parameters -- numbers learned from training data (weights, probabilities). Hyperparameters -- choices made before training (k, which kernel). Retained training data -- raw rows kept around at prediction time. KNN's k is a hyperparameter, not a fitted parameter -- this concept counts only the first and third columns, and only the first is usually what people mean by "model complexity."

05 · ARCHITECTURE / FLOW

Reusing two numbers this module already verified

Concept 03 already counted Naive Bayes at exactly 7 fitted numbers for its own d=3, K=2 CFPB example (against 19 for a full-covariance alternative it was contrasted with). Concept 06 already produced exactly 2 numbers for w and 1 for b -- w=(0.5,0.5), b=-2 -- and never needed to keep A, B, C, or D once that was fitted. Both numbers are reused here directly, not recomputed.

06 · TECHNICAL MECHANISM

The answer: 0, 7, 3, and "it depends"

KNN: 0 fitted parameters, formula n/a. Naive Bayes: 7, formula d·K + (K-1) at d=3, K=2. Linear SVM: 3, formula d+1 at d=2. Kernel SVM: no fixed formula -- n_sv·(d+1)+1, where n_sv (the number of support vectors) is itself a property of the training data, not a design choice made in advance.

07 · CONCRETE EXAMPLE

Kernel SVM's number isn't fixed -- it depends on the data

Scikit-learn's own SVM guide calls support vectors what keeps SVMs "memory efficient" (scikit-learn, Support Vector Machines) -- true relative to keeping a full kernel matrix. But "memory efficient" is relative, not a fixed constant like the linear SVM's 3. Applying this module's own d=2, N=4 convention: at n_sv=1 the count is 4 (cheaper than Naive Bayes); at n_sv=2 it ties Naive Bayes exactly at 7; at n_sv=4 (every training point becomes a support vector) it reaches 13 -- more than KNN's own 8-number raw-data cost, while being harder to read than either. That extreme is not hypothetical: Concept 07's own RBF-kernel fit on its own 4-point dataset names all four points as support vectors (support_ = [0,1,2,3]).

08 · YOUR PROJECT MAPPING

This isn't a metaphor -- it's a real attribute count

NAIVE BAYES STORES THIS LITERALLYA fitted BernoulliNB keeps feature_log_prob_ (n_classes × n_features) and class_log_prior_ (n_classes).

At d=3, K=2 that's 6+2 stored slots, with only 7 of them free (the priors sum to 1) -- exactly Concept 03's own count.

LINEAR AND KERNEL SVM DIVERGE HEREcoef_ only exists when kernel="linear"; kernel SVMs instead expose dual_coef_, support_vectors_, and intercept_.

The first is a fixed d+1 slots; the second grows with however many support vectors training happened to produce.

09 · WHAT YOU SHOULD UNDERSTAND

The takeaway

Fitted-parameter count is one proxy for complexity, not the only one

It ignores hyperparameters and effective degrees of freedom -- but it's the one this concept can count by hand, exactly.

Compact and readable often coincide, but they aren't the same property

Linear SVM is both (3 numbers, one inequality). Kernel SVM can be neither once enough points become support vectors.

KNN is the extreme case of "no model, all data"

Zero fitted numbers, but nothing is ever discarded -- the training set itself is the entire model.

Teach-back questions

  1. Why does KNN have zero fitted parameters yet still cost 8 numbers to keep, on Concept 02's own 4-point set?
  2. At what support-vector count does a kernel SVM's total exactly tie Naive Bayes' 7, and at what count does it exceed KNN's own 8?
  3. Which scikit-learn attribute exists only when an SVM's kernel is linear, and why doesn't it exist for other kernels?

01
ORIENTATION

A checkpoint, not a fourth mechanism

Concepts 01-02 predicted by voting among neighbors. Concepts 03-05 predicted by scoring Bayes'-rule likelihoods. Concept 06 predicted by margin-maximization. This concept builds no new prediction mechanism at all -- it steps back and measures the four already-built models against two shared yardsticks: how many fitted numbers each needs, and whether a person could read its decision rule off those numbers directly.

02
DEFINITION

Two proxies, defined precisely

Complexity (this concept's proxy): the count of fitted numbers a model needs, once trained, to score a brand-new point -- not counting hyperparameters chosen in advance or raw training rows retained purely for introspection. Interpretability (this concept's proxy): whether the decision rule collapses into a single closed-form expression a person could evaluate by hand, without re-scanning the training data. Both terms carry a much larger literature -- Occam's razor for complexity preference in general (Wikipedia, Occam's razor) and explainable AI for interpretability more broadly (Wikipedia, Explainable artificial intelligence) -- cited here only for vocabulary, not rebuilt.

03
SCOPE

What this concept does not claim

Fitted-parameter count is A proxy for complexity, not THE definition -- VC dimension and effective degrees of freedom are real, established alternatives, entirely out of scope here. Likewise, "fits on one line" is a proxy for interpretability, not the full interpretability toolkit -- SHAP values and global-versus-local explanations are named forward to Module 16, not built here. This concept counts only what can be counted by hand from numbers this module already produced.

04
MATHEMATICS

The four formulas

KNN: no fitted parameters. Naive Bayes: d·K + (K-1). Linear SVM: d + 1. Kernel SVM: n_sv·(d+1) + 1.

Naive Bayes' formula counts one probability per feature per class (d·K) plus (K-1) free class-prior degrees of freedom, since K priors must sum to 1. Linear SVM's formula counts w's d entries plus b. Kernel SVM's formula counts each support vector's d coordinates plus its own dual coefficient, plus one shared intercept -- derived directly from sklearn's own documented shapes for support_vectors_, dual_coef_, and intercept_ (Section 11).

05
ARCHITECTURE

The shared convention, and where it bends

Wherever a training-set size matters, this concept uses this module's own shared convention: d=2 features (x1, x2), K=2 classes, N=4 training points -- Concept 02's own KNN toy set, and the same scale used for the kernel-SVM sweep below. Naive Bayes is the one exception: its 7-vs-19 count was already independently verified in Concept 03 at its own d=3, K=2 CFPB example, and is reused exactly as published rather than silently recomputed at d=2 without a real dataset behind it.

06
TECHNICAL MECHANISM

Derivation logic, per model

KNN fits nothing at all -- scikit-learn's own framing (Section 11) is that it "simply stores instances of the training data," so its fitted-parameter count is 0 by definition, and its stored-numbers count is just N·d. Naive Bayes fits one probability per feature per class plus class priors -- d·K+(K-1). Linear SVM's optimization (Concept 06) converges on one w per feature plus one b -- d+1, independent of N once fitted. Kernel SVM's dual optimization (Section 13) converges on one dual coefficient per support vector, and the support vectors themselves must be kept because the kernel function needs their exact coordinates at prediction time -- so its count scales with n_sv, not with d alone.

07
MATHEMATICS

Resulting numbers

KNN: 0 fitted / 8 stored. Naive Bayes: 7. Linear SVM: 3. Kernel SVM: 4 (n_sv=1) → 7 (n_sv=2, ties Naive Bayes) → 10 (n_sv=3) → 13 (n_sv=4, exceeds KNN's 8).

Concept 03's own record states Naive Bayes' parameter count as "7 vs. 19 at d=3" against a full-covariance alternative. Recomputing d·K+(K-1) at d=3, K=2 here gives 3·2+1=7 -- an exact match, with no drift introduced by reusing the number in a new context.

11
DIAGNOSIS

Check 3 · sklearn's real attribute shapes

Verified live against scikit-learn's own documentation. BernoulliNB.feature_log_prob_ has shape (n_classes, n_features) and class_log_prior_ has shape (n_classes,) (scikit-learn, BernoulliNB) -- n_classes·n_features stored slots, matching d·K exactly. SVC.coef_ exists only when kernel="linear", with shape (n_classes·(n_classes-1)/2, n_features); SVC.dual_coef_ has shape (n_classes-1, n_SV); SVC.support_vectors_ has shape (n_SV, n_features); SVC.intercept_ has shape (n_classes·(n_classes-1)/2,) (scikit-learn, SVC) -- at K=2 these reduce to exactly d+1 for the linear case and n_SV·(d+1)+1 for the general case, matching Section 04's formulas exactly.

DIAGNOSIS

Check 4 · brute-force sweep

Enumerating every possible support-vector count on this module's own N=4 toy set (n_sv=1, 2, 3, 4) and computing n_sv·(d+1)+1 at each gives 4, 7, 10, 13 -- confirming the crossover points by exhaustive check, not just formula substitution: n_sv=2 ties Naive Bayes' 7 exactly, and n_sv=3 is already the first point that exceeds KNN's own 8-number raw-storage cost.

13
EVIDENCE + CONTROL

sklearn's real decision function, quoted directly

Scikit-learn's own SVM guide gives the fitted decision function verbatim: for a new sample x, decision_function computes Σi∈SV yiαiK(xi, x) + b, with the predicted class taken from its sign -- summing only over support vectors because every other point's dual coefficient αi is exactly zero (scikit-learn, Support Vector Machines). dual_coef_ holds the product yiαi per support vector, support_vectors_ holds their coordinates, and intercept_ holds b -- the exact three quantities Section 04's kernel-SVM formula counts, sourced from sklearn's own stated formula rather than derived from first principles. This concept does not re-derive what K(xi,x) itself computes for any specific kernel -- Concept 07, "The kernel trick, conceptually," already built that explicit feature-map derivation (φ(x1,x2)=(x1,x2,x1²+x2²)) and its own live RBF-kernel confirmation on its own 4-point dataset; that mapping is cited from there, not repeated here.

14
CONTRAST

One-line rule, or not

ModelFits on one readable line?Why / why not
KNNNoThe rule is a live procedure -- distance to every stored point, then a vote -- that changes the instant any stored point changes. There is no fixed formula to write down.
Naive BayesYes, but needs all 7 numbers plugged in firstBayes' rule itself is one line, but reading it requires enumerating every one of the 7 fitted probabilities beforehand.
Linear SVMYes, compactly"Escalate if x1+x2 > 4" -- a single inequality, readable at a glance, straight from Concept 06's own w and b.
Kernel SVMNoΣ y·α·K(x_i,x)+b sums over every support vector's kernel evaluation with the new point -- it cannot collapse into one readable inequality in the original x1,x2 space.

CAREER PRACTICE

Interview questions

Why does KNN have zero fitted parameters, yet isn't the most "complex" model to deploy?
Zero fitted parameters only counts what's learned from training; KNN still must retain every training point (N·d numbers) to predict anything, since it never builds an internal summary of the data at all.
At what support-vector count does a kernel SVM stop being more compact than storing the raw training set outright?
At n_sv=3 on this module's own N=4, d=2 convention: 3·3+1=10 numbers, already past KNN's own 8-number raw-storage cost -- one support vector earlier than the full training set.
Which SVC attribute exists only when kernel="linear", and why?
coef_ -- it represents the weight vector w in the original feature space, which only exists in closed form when no kernel mapping is applied. Non-linear kernels instead expose dual_coef_ and support_vectors_, since there is no single w to report.
Why is Naive Bayes' 7-parameter count reused here at d=3 instead of recomputed at this lesson's own d=2?
Concept 03 already independently verified 7 at its own real d=3, K=2 CFPB example; recomputing a fresh d=2 count without a real dataset behind it would introduce an unverified number where a verified one already exists.
22
CORRECTION

Common misconception

"Kernel methods sound more advanced, so they must pack more information into fewer numbers than a simple method like KNN."The opposite can be true. This lesson's own sweep shows a kernel SVM that keeps every training point as a support vector needs 13 numbers on a set where KNN needs only 8 -- more raw data retained (the support-vector coordinates) PLUS an extra dual coefficient per point on top. Sounding more advanced is not the same claim as being more compact.
