MODULE 07 · CONCEPT 10

Train it once. Then hand it four narratives it has never read.
A naive Bayes baseline for CFPB tagging.

Concepts 03-05 built the math on a 6-document toy set and one test document at a time. This concept runs the whole thing as an actual baseline: a 12-document CFPB-style training corpus, one Laplace-smoothed multinomial model fit to it, and four held-out narratives it never trained on -- three of which it tags correctly, and one of which it gets wrong. That 75% isn't a target number picked in advance; it's what a real Naive Bayes baseline, trained on real (if small) data, actually returns.

01 · CONCEPT FIRST

A baseline is a model you actually run, not just derive

Everything this module built -- the product rule (03), Laplace smoothing (04), the three likelihood shapes (05) -- was demonstrated one test document at a time. A baseline is different: fit it once on a training set, then hold out data it never saw and run it, unedited, to see what it actually gets right and wrong. This concept builds that baseline for CFPB narrative tagging.

First memory: A worked example scores one document by hand. A baseline is trained once and then tested cold, on documents that had no say in its training.

03 · CONCRETE EXAMPLE

The training corpus -- bigger than a toy, still hand-countable

12 CFPB-style complaint narratives, 6 per class, vocabulary {refund, delay, error, urgent, thanks, please} (n=6) -- twice the document count of Concepts 04-05's own 6-document set. Escalate narratives lean on refund/delay/error/urgent; resolve narratives lean on refund/thanks/please. Word totals: escalate refund=4, delay=5, error=5, urgent=4, thanks=0, please=0 (N=18). Resolve refund=2, thanks=8, please=8, delay=0, error=0, urgent=0 (N=18). Priors P(escalate)=P(resolve)=0.5, matching every prior worked example in this module. The CFPB itself frames real complaint narratives as "consumers' descriptions of their experiences in their own words" (Consumer Financial Protection Bureau, Consumer complaints) -- the framing this toy corpus imitates at hand-verifiable scale.

04 · TECHNICAL MECHANISM

Fitting the baseline -- the exact same formula as Concept 04

Laplace-smoothed conditionals reuse Concept 04's own formula, theta_hat=(count+alpha)/(N+alpha·n), alpha=1: every escalate conditional divides by 18+6=24, every resolve conditional divides by the same 24. Escalate: refund=5/24, delay=6/24, error=6/24, urgent=5/24, thanks=1/24, please=1/24. Resolve: refund=3/24, delay=1/24, error=1/24, urgent=1/24, thanks=9/24, please=9/24. No new smoothing mechanism -- this is Concept 04's fix, applied at this concept's own scale.

06 · IMPORTANT DISTINCTIONS

Three right, one wrong -- a real accuracy, not a chosen one

T1: P(escalate)=0.980392, correct. T2: P(escalate)=0.994475, correct. T3: P(resolve)=0.998630, correct. T4: P(escalate)=0.857143 -- the model calls escalate, but T4's true label is resolve. 3 out of 4 correct: accuracy 0.75. This number was computed by scoring the fitted model against the held-out set, not selected to make a tidy example -- a real baseline this small will make real mistakes.

07 · CONCRETE EXAMPLE

Reading the miss

T4 is just one word: "delay." Escalate's smoothed P(delay)=6/24=0.25; resolve's is 1/24=0.041667 -- "delay" is a strong escalate signal in this corpus, and it's the only word in T4. There's no "thanks" or "please" present to pull the score back toward resolve, the way there was in T1 and T2's multi-word narratives. A one-word narrative gives the model nothing to weigh the strong signal against -- the same zero-frequency-adjacent fragility Concept 04 first surfaced, now showing up as a genuine baseline error instead of a 0/0 crash.

08 · YOUR PROJECT MAPPING

A second model, run for comparison -- not a rebuild

BERNOULLINB ON THE SAME CORPUSEvery one of the four calls matches -- including the same miss on T4.

Presence/absence tallies from the identical 12 documents feed Concept 05's exact BernoulliNB formula. The verdicts don't change; the confidences do (T1: 0.998573 vs. multinomial's 0.980392) -- Concept 05's own finding, reconfirmed at this larger scale, not a new claim.

WHY MULTINOMIAL SHIPS AS THE BASELINECFPB narratives repeat words for emphasis -- multinomial can use that; Bernoulli can't.

scikit-learn frames MultinomialNB for exactly this: "data are typically represented as word vector counts" (scikit-learn, Naive Bayes). Two mentions of "urgent" carry more weight than one under multinomial; under Bernoulli they're identical. That's the explicit modeling decision this concept makes, not a default left unexamined.

09 · WHAT YOU SHOULD UNDERSTAND

The takeaway

A baseline is judged on held-out data, not training data

All four test narratives were absent from the 12-document training corpus -- the 0.75 accuracy reflects genuine generalization, not memorization.

One real mistake is expected, not a bug

T4's miss traces to a single, ambiguous word with no counter-evidence present -- a concrete, explainable failure mode, not a mysterious one.

The count-vs-presence choice still matters, even when it doesn't flip a verdict

Multinomial and Bernoulli agreed on all four calls here -- but Concept 05 already showed that agreement isn't guaranteed, and the confidence gap between them is real either way.

Teach-back questions

  1. Why is accuracy computed on the 4 held-out narratives instead of the 12 training documents?
  2. Which single word makes T4 a miss, and why doesn't the model have anything to weigh it against?
  3. Why does this concept deploy MultinomialNB rather than BernoulliNB, given that both reach the same four verdicts here?

const escalateDocs = [
  ['refund','delay','error'], ['delay','error','urgent'], ['error','urgent','refund'],
  ['urgent','refund','delay'], ['delay','error','refund'], ['urgent','delay','error'],
];
const resolveDocs = [
  ['refund','thanks','please'], ['refund','thanks','please'], ['thanks','thanks','please'],
  ['thanks','thanks','please'], ['thanks','please','please'], ['thanks','please','please'],
];
const vocab = ['refund','delay','error','urgent','thanks','please'];
function tally(docs) {
  const counts = {}; vocab.forEach(w => counts[w] = 0);
  let total = 0;
  docs.forEach(doc => doc.forEach(w => { counts[w]++; total++; }));
  return { counts, total };
}
const eT = tally(escalateDocs), rT = tally(resolveDocs);
console.log(eT); // { counts: { refund:4, delay:5, error:5, urgent:4, thanks:0, please:0 }, total: 18 }
console.log(rT); // { counts: { refund:2, delay:0, error:0, urgent:0, thanks:8, please:8 }, total: 18 }

MATHEMATICS

Fitted-parameter count, reused from Concept 08

d·K + (K-1) at d=6 (vocabulary size), K=2 (classes) = 6·2 + 1 = 13

Concept 08 established this exact formula for a Bernoulli-style count at d=3; the same formula applies unchanged to this concept's multinomial fit at d=6 -- one probability per vocabulary word per class (12), plus one free class-prior degree of freedom (1), totaling 13. This is the same accounting Concept 08 used for KNN (0), the linear SVM (3, d+1 at d=2) and the kernel SVM (data-dependent), reused here rather than invented fresh.

Node verification 2 -- all four posteriors, full precision

JAVASCRIPT · SCORES THE HELD-OUT SET AGAINST THE FITTED MODEL FROM SECTION 07 
function pWord(counts, total, w) { return (counts[w] + 1) / (total + 6); }
function score(words) {
  let pE = 1, pR = 1;
  words.forEach(w => { pE *= pWord(eT.counts, eT.total, w); pR *= pWord(rT.counts, rT.total, w); });
  const jE = 0.5 * pE, jR = 0.5 * pR, total = jE + jR;
  return { postE: jE / total, postR: jR / total, pred: jE >= jR ? 'escalate' : 'resolve' };
}
console.log(score(['refund','delay','urgent'])); // postE: 0.9803921568627452, pred: 'escalate'
console.log(score(['error','urgent','error']));  // postE: 0.994475138121547,  pred: 'escalate'
console.log(score(['thanks','please','thanks'])); // postR: 0.9986301369863013, pred: 'resolve'
console.log(score(['delay']));                    // postE: 0.8571428571428571, pred: 'escalate'
// -- 3 of 4 predictions match the true label in Section 10; T4 does not.

DIAGNOSIS

Diagnosing the miss -- why T4 specifically

T4 is a single word, "delay." In this corpus, P(delay|escalate)=6/24=0.25 versus P(delay|resolve)=1/24=0.041667 -- a roughly 6:1 tilt toward escalate on that word alone. T1 and T2 also contain escalate-leaning words, but each has three words' worth of evidence to combine; T4 has exactly one, so there is nothing else in the product to counteract that tilt. This is not a bug in the smoothing or the independence assumption -- it's the predictable consequence of a short, single-word narrative carrying a strong-but-wrong signal with no competing evidence present, the same fragility Concept 04's zero-frequency problem first exposed, now surfacing as a real classification error instead of an unclassifiable 0/0.

EVIDENCE + CONTROL

The modeling decision, made explicitly

Both variants reach the same four calls here, so this concept's choice of multinomial over Bernoulli isn't forced by this particular test set -- it's a decision made on what the feature actually represents, the same standard Concept 05 named: "match the variant to the feature type." CFPB narratives are free text where a word can plausibly repeat for emphasis (a consumer writing "urgent urgent" is not the same signal as writing "urgent" once); scikit-learn's own framing of MultinomialNB as built for data where "the data are typically represented as word vector counts" (scikit-learn, Naive Bayes) matches that directly. BernoulliNB would silently discard repetition the moment it collapsed every count above 1 to a single "present" flag. Multinomial ships as this concept's deployed baseline for that reason -- Bernoulli's parallel run in Sections 16-17 exists to check the decision, not to replace it.

MECHANICS

How the console computes both scores at once

Each slider word w with count c contributes P_multi(w|class)^c to the multinomial product (c=0 contributes 1, i.e. nothing) using Section 08's fitted conditionals, and c>0 ? P_bern(w|class) : (1-P_bern(w|class)) to the Bernoulli product using Section 16's fitted conditionals -- every vocabulary word enters the Bernoulli product regardless of whether its slider is at 0, since Bernoulli scores absence too. Both products get the 0.5 prior and are normalized the same way as every worked example above. The four load buttons set all six sliders to reproduce Section 10's exact narratives; dragging any slider afterward moves back into free exploration, and the verdict line states each model's call without asserting a "true" label unless one of the four buttons was the last thing clicked.

CAREER PRACTICE

Interview questions

Why is this concept's accuracy computed on 4 held-out narratives rather than the 12 training documents?
Scoring training data only measures memorization, not generalization; a baseline's real accuracy has to come from documents it never saw fit, which is exactly why T4's miss is meaningful rather than a modeling error.
What specifically causes T4 ("delay") to be misclassified, and is it a flaw in the smoothing?
T4 is a single word with a strong escalate-leaning conditional (0.25 vs. 0.041667) and no other word present to offset it. It's not a smoothing flaw -- smoothing already prevented a 0/0 crash; it's the predictable outcome of too little evidence in a very short narrative.
Why does this concept choose MultinomialNB for deployment when BernoulliNB reaches the same four verdicts?
Because the feature type -- free-text word repetition carrying emphasis -- matches multinomial's count-based likelihood, per Concept 05's "match the variant to the feature type" rule and scikit-learn's own framing of MultinomialNB for word-count data; agreement on this particular test set doesn't change what the underlying feature actually represents.
Why doesn't this concept claim KNN or an SVM would do better or worse on this same corpus?
Neither was run on this text data -- Section 21's comparison only reuses each model's own already-published fitted-parameter formulas and decision-rule shapes from Concepts 01, 02, 06 and 07, not a new benchmark.

CORRECTION

Common misconception

"A worked baseline with a real mistake in it must mean something was set up wrong."A single mistake on 4 held-out narratives, traced to one short, ambiguous document, is exactly what a small, honest baseline looks like -- not a sign of a broken pipeline. Reporting 0.75 accuracy with a diagnosed cause (Section 15) is more trustworthy than reporting a suspiciously perfect 1.0 on a test set this small, which would be far more likely to mean the test narratives were too easy or, worse, leaked into training.
